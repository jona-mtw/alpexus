# **Murphy's Law**



"Anything that can go wrong will go wrong."



Well its not an actual law of the universe, like say the Theory of Relativity, but because people naturally remember negative experiences in their lives more strongly, the phenomenon feels so real. Forgetting your homework the one day that one teacher who never checks the homework checks it. Your phone battery dying when it's really important. Waking up really early during the holidays on accident, but being tired every day you wake up for school. That last one is a big one for me.



It may seem like if everything bad is bound to happen, why bother trying at all? Despite it sounding seemingly pessimistic, it is actually a call for us to prepare for the worst. Originally coined by the aerospace engineer Capt. Ed Murphy, it was meant as a call to anticipate potential failures that could happen instead of the anticipation of inevitable bad outcomes. And while it started as a means to prepare for potential design failures, it now widely referenced for everyday mishaps. Instead of seeing it as something that can't be avoided, and there's no point in trying, the creator of the phrase meant it as a way to prepare, double check and have backup plans for anything important.



Another way of thinking about Murphy's law is, "anything that can happen, will happen," including the positives and the negatives. This outlook of Murphy's law shows us that we should not only be prepared for the worst, but also be hopeful for the best outcome.



As for you who have watched Interstellar, directed by Christopher Nolan, Murphy's Law may ring a bell. It's a recurring theme throughout the entire movie, and it reinterprets Murphy's Law in a similar, a more optimistic way. Murph, the daughter of the main character Cooper, asks why she was named after a seemingly bad phenomenon. Cooper responds with, “Murphy’s law doesn’t mean that something bad will happen. It means that whatever can happen, will happen.” And this  line becomes really important throughout the entire movie. Of course, bad things happen, as with nearly any movie. Cooper is forced to sacrifice himself by sending himself into a black hole, which he knows would end in his death. But in the black hole, he is able to communicate with his daughter on Earth, and gives the answer to the gravity equation needed to save Earth. And even though Cooper somehow survives and makes it back to Earth, due to time dilation, by the time Cooper sees Murph, she is double his age and on her deathbed. He had missed all of her life, and yet was able to help save the Earth.



Interstellar argues that Murphy's Law isn't pessimistic - its about possibility. Both the awful and the miraculous outcomes are possible. And Interstellar does an awesome job at conveying this.



We can't control everything that happens in our lives. Plans fail, mistakes happen, and sometimes life feels unfair. But Murphy's Law, at least for me, reminds us that the possibility of certain types of outcomes works in both ways: both the good and the bad are possible. If disaster is possible, so is success.
